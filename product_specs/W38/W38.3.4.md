## Objective

Handle the move the business spec actually cares about: a member moving to a team
in a **different department**, where the fields we display about them change.
Plus tell the user when a move is refused, instead of silently doing nothing.

## Background

From the Phase 1 spec:

> Implement this for within the same department and for different departments.

and, from the VIEW section:

> Although all members have the same fields, not all members have all the same
> fields visible. For example, members in the development department have a field
> for GitHub username, but members outside of the Development Department do not.

So moving someone from Team Whale (Development) to the Accounting Team (Finance)
is not just a relabel — the cell renders differently afterwards. `TeamLayout`
already branches on `team.kind === "development"` for the team-level stats row,
but the **member-level** fields are rendered unconditionally. That is the gap.

Also: `moveMemberToTeam` carries `proficiency` over. A development proficiency
score means nothing in the Finance department. We are not fixing that this week —
the proficiency refactor is a whole feature in `features_TODO.md` — but the move
should not *display* a stale development score in a Finance row.

## Files you will touch

- `src/components/SpreadsheetLayout.tsx`
- `utils/model/operations.ts` (extend `canMoveToTeam` only)

## What to change

1. **Field visibility by department.** In `TeamLayout`, gate the member-level
   fields on the team's department rather than rendering all of them always. At
   minimum, GitHub username and the proficiency line show only for teams in the
   Development department. Do not hardcode a list of team names — derive it from
   the department the team sits under. (`team.kind` is already there and may be
   enough; if it is, say so in a comment rather than adding a second mechanism.)

2. **Verify a cross-department move end to end.** Drag a member from a
   Development team to a Finance or Marketing team. They should appear in the
   destination with the destination's field set, not the source's.

3. **Extend `canMoveToTeam`** with the rules that only matter across departments.
   Decide which of these you agree with, implement the ones you keep, and write
   one line each for the ones you reject and why:
   - Moving a member who is a `teamLead` out of their team leaves that team
     without a lead. Refuse? Warn? Allow?
   - A member already holding a current position on the destination team cannot
     be moved onto it again.
   - A board-level role (`President`, `Vice President`) being dragged onto a
     development team — sensible, or nonsense?

   There is no single right answer. What we need is that a decision was made
   deliberately and written down.

4. **Surface rejections.** When `canMoveToTeam` returns `ok: false`, show the
   `reason` to the user. A toast, an inline banner at the top of the page, or a
   message on the team that refused — your choice, but it must be visible without
   opening DevTools, and it must go away on its own or be dismissible.

5. **Signal refusal during the drag, not only after.** While dragging over a team
   that will refuse the drop, show a "not allowed" state rather than the normal
   highlight. Remember the constraint from W38.3.2: `dragover` cannot read the
   payload. You will need the dragged member in React state, set at `dragstart`.
   This is why that spec asked you to plan for it.

## How to approach it

- Item 5 is the one with a real design decision in it. Get 1–4 working first;
  they are mechanical.
- Test the awkward cases explicitly: move a team lead, move a member who holds
  two positions, move someone into a team they are already on.
- Keep the rejection UI simple. A fixed-position banner with a `useState` message
  and a `setTimeout` to clear it is roughly fifteen lines. Do not add a toast
  library.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GitHub username and proficiency are visible for members rendered in
      Development teams and hidden for members rendered in non-development teams.
- [ ] Field visibility is derived from the team's department, not a hardcoded
      list of team names.
- [ ] A member dragged from a Development team to a non-development team renders
      with the destination's field set immediately after the drop.
- [ ] `canMoveToTeam` refuses at least one cross-department case, with a
      human-readable reason.
- [ ] Every rule you considered is recorded in a comment — kept ones with their
      implementation, rejected ones with a reason.
- [ ] A refused drop shows a visible message; it does not fail silently.
- [ ] Dragging over a team that would refuse shows a distinct "not allowed"
      state.
- [ ] No move ever removes a `Position` from `positionHistory`.
- [ ] `npm run build` passes.
