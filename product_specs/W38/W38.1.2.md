## Objective

Write a pure function that turns the flat `members: Membership[]` array into the
nested `OperationsSector → Department → Cluster → Team` tree the spreadsheet
renders, so the UI stops inventing fake teams.

## Background

Open `src/pages/members.tsx`. It imports the real `members` array and then
throws it away: it hand-builds a `Team` called `"Team Lemon"`, puts *every*
member on it, builds a second team called `"Team NonDev"` with the same members,
and then a department containing `[team, nonDevTeam, team]` — the same team
object twice. Every member appears everywhere. That was fine as scaffolding, but
we cannot build drag-and-drop or a hover card on top of it.

The real data is flat on purpose. Each `Membership` has a `positionHistory`, and
each current `Position` in it carries `operationsSector`, `department`, `team`
and `cluster` as plain strings. We keep it flat so there are no circular
references between members. The nesting is a **view concern** — something we
compute for rendering, not something we store.

So: one function, `members[] → OperationsSector[]`.

## Files you will touch

- `utils/model/organization.ts` (new file)
- `src/components/SpreadsheetLayout.tsx` (cluster rendering)

## What to change

1. Create `utils/model/organization.ts`.

2. Export a function `buildOrganization(members: Membership[]): OperationsSector[]`.

   For every member, look at their **current** positions only — there is already
   a `currentPositions(member)` helper exported from `types.ts`, use it. A member
   with two current positions (say Developer on Team Whale *and* Treasurer in
   Finance) must appear in **both** places in the tree. That is the whole reason
   the Formations sheet is hard to maintain by hand.

   Group by, in order: `position.operationsSector` → `position.department` →
   `position.cluster` (if present) → `position.team` (if present).

3. Decide where a member lands when their position has some of those fields
   missing. Write your rules as comments at the top of the function. Suggested
   rules, but argue for your own if you disagree:
   - `team` present, `cluster` present → team goes in that cluster inside that
     department.
   - `team` present, no `cluster` → team hangs directly off the department
     (`department.teams`).
   - no `team` → the member sits on the department itself
     (`department.members`), not on any team.
   - no `department` → skip this position for now, and count it. Return or log
     the count so we know how much data is incomplete.

4. Fill in the `Team` fields you cannot derive from a member: `kind` is
   `"development"` when the department is Development and `"non-development"`
   otherwise. `teamLead` is whichever member on that team holds the role
   `"Team Lead"`, `"Primary Lead"` or `"Secondary Lead"`. **A team may have no
   lead in the data** — handle that; do not use `!` to assert one exists the way
   `members.tsx` currently does.

5. In `SpreadsheetLayout.tsx`, replace the `TODO(W38.1.2)` comment from W38.1.1
   with real cluster rendering: a department renders its clusters (each cluster
   showing its name and its teams) and then its direct teams. Add a
   `ClusterLayout` function following the same shape as `TeamLayout` and
   `DepartmentLayout`.

## How to approach it

- A `Map` keyed by name is much easier than repeatedly `.find()`-ing through
  arrays. Build maps as you go, then convert to arrays at the end.
- Write the function signature and return an empty array first. Confirm it
  compiles. Then fill it in one level at a time: sectors, then departments, then
  teams, then clusters.
- Check your work against `utils/model/data.ts` — it has real names in it. Print
  the tree with `console.log(JSON.stringify(tree, null, 2))` and eyeball it
  against the Crew Formations doc.
- `buildOrganization` must be **pure**: same input, same output, no mutation of
  the `members` array, no `Date.now()`, no imports from `src/`. This matters,
  because in Phase 2 this exact function moves to the backend.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `utils/model/organization.ts` exists and exports `buildOrganization`.
- [ ] The function does not mutate its argument. (Test: snapshot
      `JSON.stringify(members)` before and after; they match.)
- [ ] A member with two current positions in different departments appears in
      both departments in the output.
- [ ] A member with no current position appears nowhere in the tree and does not
      crash the function.
- [ ] Teams with no identifiable lead do not throw.
- [ ] Your grouping rules are written as comments at the top of the function.
- [ ] `ClusterLayout` renders, and a department shows both its clusters and its
      direct teams.
- [ ] `npm run build` passes.
